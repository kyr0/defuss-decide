# A smaller text decision bundle: fp16 now, int4 with a repair step

Requested 2026-11-08 by the maintainer: reduce the size of the vela3-fast backbone and its typed decision head (`decision/fast`) without losing much quality, int4 if done well. The order below was agreed in the conversation: a lossless fp16 bundle first, then an evaluation set and gate, a sensitivity sweep, GPTQ int4, a repair loop by distillation, and product-quantized embeddings only as an add-on to that loop. The vision decision head and the shared backbone for the task adapters follow in their own plans.

Tick a milestone `- [x]` when its proof passes, then run `verify` on its scope.

## What is known

- VERIFIED: the checkpoint has 380.4M FP32 parameters: 327.2M in 168 bias-free linear matrices (24 layers × q, k, v, o, gate, up, down), 50.3M in the untied 49,160 × 1024 token table, 2.9M in the decision head (`w_q`, `w_o`, `w_p`, `cls_mlp`, `norm`, `log_tau`) (safetensors header of `checkpoints/fast/model.safetensors`).
- VERIFIED: the head scores options by cosine similarity divided by τ = 0.064 plus an MLP (`checkpoints/fast/vela3_encoder.py:DecisionEncoder._logits`, `config.json` `decision.tau`). A change of the cosine changes the option's logit about 16 times as much, so small representation errors tip answers near the decision boundary.
- VERIFIED: the probe in `tmp/quant-probe/` (PyTorch, weights quantized and dequantized, FP32 compute) compared each variant with FP32 on the 56 text cases of `tests/fixtures/decide-cases.json` (FP32: 52 as labelled) and on 131 held-out synthetic inputs; GPTQ used Hessians from 131 other inputs:

  | Variant | Labelled correct | Flips labelled / held-out | Held-out mean shift | Held-out max shift |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | fp16, all weights | 52 | 0 / 0 | 0.0001 | 0.001 |
  | Linear int8, per channel (the task q8 method) | 52 | 0 / 0 | 0.003 | 0.02 |
  | Linear int4, per channel | 52 | 2 / 7 | 0.043 | 0.83 |
  | Linear int4, symmetric, groups of 16 (the task q4 method) | 52 | 2 / 2 | 0.023 | 0.38 |
  | Linear int4, groups of 32, min-max | 53 | 1 / 2 | 0.018 | 0.21 |
  | Linear GPTQ int4, groups of 32 | 52 | 0 / 2 | 0.013 | 0.16 |
  | Embeddings int4, groups of 32 | 53 | 1 / 1 | 0.009 | 0.08 |
  | Embeddings PQ, 2 bits per dimension, no refit | 51 | 1 / 9 | 0.052 | 0.90 |
  | GPTQ int4 g32 + embeddings int4 g32 | 52 | 0 / 2 | 0.013 | 0.21 |
  | Linear GPTQ int4, per channel | 51 | 5 / 5 | 0.023 | 0.27 |
  | GPTQ int4 per channel + embeddings int4 per row | 51 | 5 / 6 | 0.033 | 0.36 |

  A flip is a changed top option against FP32, not necessarily a wrong answer; a shift is the largest change of an option's probability in one answer. The sets are small and narrow: the 56 cases are a hand-written smoke set, the synthetic inputs come from this repository's documents and generated records.
- VERIFIED: the published task q8 and q4 bundles leave the token table in FP32 (`python/quantize_tasks.py` docstring), about 192 MB of each 543 MB or 466 MB bundle.
- VERIFIED: the v0.1 decision gate (`python/quantize.py`) allows a probability error of 0.02 on 5 fixtures; weight-only int8 failed it with 0.0206 (`reports/quantization-rejected.json`).
- VERIFIED: `python/quantize_tasks.py:quantize_graph` already writes symmetric int4 in blocks along the reduction axis with standard `DequantizeLinear` (opset 21), and those task q4 bundles pass in Node.js, Bun, Chromium and WebKit (`reports/tasks/*-q4*.json`). They use groups of 16 with one FP32 scale each (`reports/quantization-tasks.json`), so a weight costs 6 bits instead of 4; FP16 scales on groups of 32 with 4-bit zero points cost 4.6 bits.
- VERIFIED: every text task adapter is LoRA rank 64 on all 168 matrices plus retrained top layers and its own head, trained on the frozen decision backbone (`checkpoints/fast/vela3_config.json`). The bundles are not hosted yet (`.vae/MEMORY.md`), so the layout can still change.

## Open questions and their probes

- UNKNOWN: whether Vela3's training data is published. Probe: the model card and repository files of `vllm-sr/vela3-fast` at the pinned revision. Without it, the repair data is built from public datasets (milestone 1).
- VERIFIED (probe 2026-11-08, `tmp/probe-split/`): `MatMulNBits` (4 bits, groups of 32, zero points) is exact in Node.js on CPU and WebGPU and in Chromium and WebKit on WASM and WebGPU. Blocked `DequantizeLinear` with 4-bit zero points is wrong on the Node.js CPU at optimization level `extended` and above (exact below), so the graphs use `MatMulNBits` directly. `GatherBlockQuantized` has no WASM kernel; a lookup of packed bytes unpacked with standard operators is exact everywhere. A graph whose weights come from two data files loads everywhere, from URLs or from in-memory buffers; ONNX Runtime rejects a data file outside the graph's folder by path, even through a symlink. The IR version (8, 10, 13) does not change the optimized graph.
- UNKNOWN: how long distillation through the 380M model takes on this machine (MPS or CPU). Probe: time 50 training steps before milestone 4.
- Maintainer decision: the quality gate's thresholds (milestone 1) and the datasets with their licenses.

## Runtime format for int4

| Option | For | Against | Order | Cheapest falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blocked `DequantizeLinear` (standard, opset 21) | VERIFIED: runs in all four runtimes for the task q4 bundles; any ONNX runtime reads it | HYPOTHESIS: the runtime folds it to FP32 at load, so only the download shrinks | 1 if the probe fails | a session's memory after load, against FP32 |
| `MatMulNBits` (ONNX Runtime contrib) | int4 weights stay int4 in memory, dedicated kernels | contrib operator, ONNX Runtime only; support on both EPs unknown | 1 if the probe passes | a two-node graph in Node.js, Bun, Chromium and WebKit, WASM and WebGPU |

The project runs only on ONNX Runtime (`onnxruntime-node` and `onnxruntime-web` 1.31.1 in `package.json`), so a contrib operator costs no portability that the project uses.

### M4: repair loop (weight-space programming), requested 2026-11-09

The shared backbone file stays as it is: the four validated text task bundles read it, so a repair may change only what the decision bundle owns. VERIFIED: the decision bundle's own file holds its 49 normalization vectors and its head (`reports/quantization-gptq.json`, counts `fp32: 60`).

- [x] 1. `python/repair_decision.py`: the student is the q4 decision model as published (int4 weights and token table decoded from `models/backbone/fast/q4/backbone.onnx.data` through the q4 graph, so the repair targets the exact shared codes), plus a trainable rank-16 correction per backbone matrix (a LoRA branch, like the task adapters), with the normalization vectors and the head trainable. The FP32 model is the teacher. Loss: KL divergence of the option probabilities plus the relative squared error of the final hidden states on every token, which anchors the representation the head reads. Data, label-free: BoolQ train (google/boolq at revision 35b264d0, CC BY-SA 3.0) as yes/no and choice questions, generated records, and the calibration half of the repository paragraphs. Model selection on half of BoolQ validation; test on the other half, the repository held-out paragraphs, the 56 labelled cases and the 5 fixtures. Runs on MPS when present, detached, with checkpoints.
- [ ] 2. Export: the repaired model goes through the same ONNX exporter (`FastDecision`), then `quantize_gptq.rewrite` maps the backbone matrices to the existing shared tensors (byte-checked against the file), the correction matrices to int8 per channel in the decision's own file. Gate (proposed, for the maintainer to adjust): every fixture keeps FP32's option, and at most 1 % of the held-out decisions flip. Proof: ONNX Runtime reproduces the PyTorch student; `node scripts/verify-js.ts` on the bundle; the gate figures in `reports/quantization-repair.json`. Docs: what was trained, on which data, and the measured gain.
- Result (2026-11-09): VERIFIED: the loop runs but does not reach the gate, so nothing was installed and `decision/fast/q4` stays unvalidated. On the 300 selection questions the unrepaired q4 model flips 8 decisions (mean largest probability shift 0.0169). The corrections at learning rate 2e-5 bring that to 6 flips and 0.0164 after 1,000 steps, and no further by step 4,000; at 2e-4 the training diverged. A 1,600-step probe with trainable group scales (learning rate 1e-4) reached 5 flips and 0.0164, against 5 flips and 0.0161 for the corrections alone. Selection picks the best state, so its 2 % flip rate is the optimistic estimate against the gate's 1 %. The full run was stopped at step 4,500 by the memory guard when another job started (logs `var/log/swarm/repair-probe.log`, `var/log/swarm/repair-full.log`).

### M5: product-quantized embeddings (add-on)

- [ ] 1. In the same loop: PQ of the token table (2 bits per dimension, 4-dim subvectors, 256 centroids), first fitted without data (a learned rotation, residual codebooks), then the codebooks refitted by distillation with an anchor that keeps every row near its original vector. Kept only if it reaches the drift of int4 in groups of 32 on held-out text; it saves 16 to 22 MB of about 0.22 GB. Proof: the M1 gate, plus drift on held-out text in languages and domains absent from the repair data.

## Later

- The shared backbone: one quantized backbone plus each task's LoRA weights, retrained layers and head, instead of a merged backbone per task bundle. A decision plus three tasks would fall from about 3.1 GB (1,455 MB + 3 × 543 MB) to roughly 0.5 GB. It needs the LoRA branch unmerged in the graphs and a check that the adapters still hold on a quantized backbone.

## Risks and rollback

- Every bundle stays behind `validated: true`; a candidate that fails the gate is not published, and FP32 and w16a32 remain.
- The repair data decides what the repaired model is good at. Held-out sources and the anchor set guard the general-purpose claim; a loss on any held-out set blocks publication.
